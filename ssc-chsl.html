SSC CHSL Preparation Guide

Last updated: 31 October 2026

The Combined Higher Secondary Level (CHSL) examination is conducted by the Staff Selection Commission for candidates who have passed Class 12 (10+2). Preparation is built around a few sections that you can master with consistent practice.

Sections to cover

  • General Intelligence / Reasoning – verbal and non-verbal reasoning.
  • General Awareness – static GK, polity, history, geography, basic science and current affairs.
  • Quantitative Aptitude – arithmetic and basic mathematics at the level of Class 10–12.
  • English Language – grammar, vocabulary, comprehension and writing-based skills.

Later stages may include skill or typing tests depending on the post, and the details change by notification. Read the current notice carefully before you plan.

Weekly plan (example)

DayFocus
Mon / ThuQuantitative Aptitude: one topic + 30 timed questions
Tue / FriReasoning: two topics + a short sectional test
Wed / SatEnglish: grammar rule, vocabulary, one comprehension passage
Daily (20 min)General Awareness: notes revision and current-affairs summary
SunFull or half mock test and error analysis

Practice approach

Start with accuracy, then add speed. Solve 20 questions slowly and check each one, then repeat the same set under a timer. Keep a notebook for formulae, tricky rules and words you missed, and read it every Sunday.

Time and negative marking

In a one-hour paper with four sections, give each section a time budget (for example 15 minutes) during mocks and learn to move on when a question is eating your time. If the paper has negative marking, attempt a question only when you can eliminate at least two options by reasoning. Track your attempts, accuracy and net score after every mock; accuracy usually matters more than raw attempts.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Memorising GK without revision – plan a weekly recap.
  • Skipping basics of Mathematics and jumping to shortcuts.
  • Not practising on screen if the exam is computer-based.
