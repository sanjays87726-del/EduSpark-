Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 September 2026

This page explains what information EduSpark collects and how it is used. Using the app means you agree to this policy.

Information we collect

  • Account information — if you sign in with Google, we receive your name, email address, and profile photo.
  • Phone number — collected for certain features that require verification.
  • Device identifier — used to support features like the doubts section without requiring you to sign in, and to keep your saved content in sync.
  • Notification data — if you enable notifications, a device token is used to deliver study reminders and updates.

How we use this information

  • To sync your notes, progress, and profile across your devices
  • To personalise your profile (your name and photo)
  • To send study reminders and important updates, if notifications are enabled
  • To operate core features such as quizzes and doubt-solving

How we store information

Data is stored securely using Google Firebase, our backend service provider. We do not maintain a separate database of your personal details beyond what these features need.

Sharing of information

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it with advertisers. Limited data may be processed by Firebase (Google) as our service provider, under Google's own privacy terms.

Students and young users

Since EduSpark is used mainly by school students, we collect only the minimum information needed to run the app. We encourage students to use it with a parent or guardian's awareness, especially when signing in with a Google account.

Your choices

  • You can sign out of your account at any time.
  • You can turn off notifications from your device or browser settings.
  • You can request deletion of your account data by reaching out on our Contact page.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as EduSpark adds new features. The "last updated" date at the top will always reflect the latest version.

This policy describes our practices in plain language and isn't a substitute for legal advice. If EduSpark needs to meet specific legal requirements (such as India's data protection rules), it's worth having this reviewed by a professional.
