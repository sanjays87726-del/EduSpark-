CBSE Class 12 Board Exam Study Guide

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Class 12 results matter for college admissions and for many entrance exams, so a steady plan is more useful than last-minute cramming. This guide gives a structure that works for Science, Commerce and Humanities students.

Plan by stream

StreamTypical subjectsWhere marks are usually won
SciencePhysics, Chemistry, Mathematics or Biology, EnglishNumerical practice, derivations, reaction mechanisms, labelled diagrams
CommerceAccountancy, Business Studies, Economics, Mathematics (optional), EnglishFormat-based accounting problems, definitions, case studies
HumanitiesHistory, Political Science, Geography, Economics, languagesStructured long answers, maps, examples and keywords

Subject combinations differ between schools, so use the list your school has registered for you.

How to prepare

  1. Anchor everything on NCERT. Most conceptual questions come from the NCERT text. Finish the exercises before moving to reference books.
  2. Use the official curriculum and sample paper. Check chapter-wise marks distribution so that you spend time in proportion to marks.
  3. Keep an error notebook. Every time you get a question wrong, write the question, the correct method and the reason you made the mistake. Review this notebook every week.
  4. Do not neglect practicals and projects. They are scored internally and are the easiest marks to secure with regular work.
  5. Full-length practice. In the last eight weeks, solve one full paper every two or three days in exam time, then analyse it.

If you are also preparing for entrance exams

Strong NCERT fundamentals help in entrance tests such as JEE, NEET or CUET, but those exams have their own syllabus and style. Plan separate time for them and do not compromise board preparation.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the writing practice and only reading solutions.
  • Not learning the answer-presentation style: headings, steps, units, diagrams.
  • Comparing your pace with others instead of tracking your own weekly progress.

Official sources – always confirm here

  • CBSE official website – circulars, exam dates, scheme of assessment
  • CBSE Academic – curriculum, syllabus, sample papers and marking schemes
  • NCERT – free official textbooks
