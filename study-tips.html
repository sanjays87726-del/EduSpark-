How to Study with Previous Year Questions and Quizzes

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Previous year questions (PYQs) and quizzes are the fastest way to learn what an exam really asks. But they only help if you use them in the right order. This page describes a method you can apply to any board or competitive exam.

The six-step method

  1. Learn first, test second. Read the chapter or topic once before attempting questions on it. Testing a topic you have never studied only teaches you to guess.
  2. Attempt without help. Answer from memory, in writing where possible. Mark any answer you are unsure about, even if it turns out correct.
  3. Check immediately. Read the explanation for every wrong or doubtful answer. The explanation is where the learning happens.
  4. Write in an error log. For each mistake note the topic, why you were wrong (concept gap, careless error, time pressure, misread question) and the correct method.
  5. Revise on a schedule. Redo the error-log questions after one day, one week and one month.
  6. Simulate the exam. Every week, take a timed paper under real conditions and track your score, accuracy and time per section.

Using PYQs well

  • Sort questions by topic to see which chapters carry the most weight.
  • Notice the language and format of questions – examiners often repeat styles even when the numbers change.
  • Do not memorise answers; the same concept will be asked in a different way.

Using quizzes well

  • Use short quizzes (10–20 questions) right after studying a topic and again a week later.
  • Prefer practice mode with explanations while learning, and timed mock mode when you are exam-ready.
  • Stop guessing: if negative marking applies, practise skipping questions you cannot reason out.

Staying consistent

Ten focused minutes every day is better than five hours once a week. Sleep, a short walk and regular meals improve memory and concentration more than another late-night session.
